# Commenting playbook

Goal: every comment should make the creator (and their readers) think "this person knows the game."
Build thought leadership and followers through comments that are useful, specific and short.

## Topic gate (check BEFORE writing)

Only comment when the post is about: GTM, product marketing, positioning, sales, demand gen,
growth, PLG, RevOps, pricing, content/brand for B2B, founder lessons on building and scaling a business,
AI applied to GTM work.

**Never comment** (skip the creator, use a backup) when the post is about, or leans on:
- religion or faith
- politics, government, elections, policy debates, political figures, wars and conflicts
- race, ethnicity, immigration debates, gender wars, or other identity controversy
- tragedy, death, illness, grief, layoffs of the author personally, mental health crises
- personal life news (weddings, babies, birthdays), job-change announcements, hiring posts
- giveaways ("comment X to get my guide"), polls with no substance, pure event promos
- reposts of someone else's content, or anything you can't fully read (video-only, image-only with no text)
- anything older than 5 days

When unsure, skip. A skipped post costs nothing; a bad comment costs reputation.

## Comment types (rotate; no more than ~40% of one type per run)

1. **Specific question**. Ask about the one detail that makes the idea work or break.
   It must be a question the author can answer from experience, not a request for free consulting.
   - "When you moved the pricing page CTA to 'talk to sales', did demo quality drop or hold? I've seen the volume go up but the show rate fall."
2. **Add a missing angle**. One insight the post did not cover: an edge case, a second-order effect, a "this works until X".
   - "One thing I'd add: this breaks once you have two buyers. The champion loves the narrative, but finance needs the same story told in cost terms."
3. **Proof point / research**. A real, verifiable data point or named study that supports or challenges the post.
   Only cite things you are sure exist (e.g. B2B Institute 95:5 rule, Gong/Pavilion/Bain/McKinsey/Forrester/Gartner
   published findings, well-known public company examples). If you are not certain of the number and source, use type 1 or 2 instead. **Never invent stats.**
   - "This lines up with the B2B Institute's 95:5 finding: only about 5% of buyers are in market at any time, so the 'not now' crowd is most of your future pipeline."
4. **Practitioner example**. A short "here's what we saw" from building GTM at a B2B SaaS / edtech company (Knorish / Edbound context).
   Keep it believable and modest. No made-up metrics; describe direction, not precise numbers, unless the user gave them in `feedback.md`.

## Style rules

- Simple English. Short sentences. Write like a smart operator talking, not a consultant.
- 25–70 words. Two to four sentences. One idea per comment.
- Reference something specific from the post (a phrase, a step, a number) so it can't be pasted anywhere else.
- Open with the substance. Banned openers: "Great post", "Love this", "So true", "This is gold", "Spot on", "100%", "Couldn't agree more", "Thanks for sharing".
- No emojis (at most one, rarely). No hashtags. No links. No @-tagging others. No self-promotion or pitching.
- Don't flatter, don't lecture, don't "well actually". Respectful disagreement is fine and often the best comment.
- Avoid AI tells: no em dashes, no "game-changer", "delve", "leverage", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world", "it's not X, it's Y" constructions, no triple lists.
- Vary sentence openers and structure across the run. Read the run's earlier comments before writing the next one.

## Quality check (score each draft; post only if all are yes)

1. Topic gate passed?
2. Could this comment only exist under this post?
3. Would the author want to reply?
4. Is every fact true and every source real?
5. Is it simple enough for a non-native English reader?
6. Does it sound like a person, not a bot?
